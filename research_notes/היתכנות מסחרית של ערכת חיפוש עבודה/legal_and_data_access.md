# Legal, platform and data-access constraints on a commercial AI job-search / auto-apply product (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: this is research, not legal advice. Findings are dated. Where a source is a vendor blog or a secondary aggregator rather than a primary source, that is flagged. US law here is mostly Ninth Circuit / N.D. Cal. case law (LinkedIn's and Amazon's home forum); EU and Israeli law differ and are noted separately.

## 1. LinkedIn: terms, case law (hiQ, Proxycurl, ProAPIs), enforcement against auto-apply, official APIs

### Takeaway
LinkedIn's contract terms ban scraping and bots, and its own help pages name browser extensions and plug-ins that "automate activity". Courts have held those terms enforceable against logged-in users (hiQ, Nov 2022). LinkedIn sues scrapers and wins injunctions (hiQ 2022, Proxycurl 2025) and has more suits pending (ProAPIs, Oct 2025). Under the CFAA, scraping logged-out public pages is likely not "unauthorized access" (hiQ, 9th Cir. 2022), and terms you never accepted may not bind logged-out scraping (Meta v. Bright Data, Jan 2024). There is no LinkedIn API that gives job-seeker apps job search or apply. Job and apply APIs are only for vetted ATS partners.

### Cited Findings
**User Agreement and help-center rules (current version effective Nov 3, 2025)**
- The User Agreement in effect from **November 3, 2025**, §8.2 "Don'ts", says you may not: "Develop, support or use software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes (such as crawlers, browser plugins and add-ons or any other technology) to scrape or copy the Services"; "Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages, create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement"; "Override any security feature or bypass or circumvent any access controls or use limits of the Services". — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- Governing law: users in "Designated Countries" (EU/EEA/Switzerland) are under Irish law (Irish courts or their home-country courts). Everyone else is under California law, with venue in Santa Clara County. — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- Help Center, "Prohibited software and extensions": "we don't permit the use of any third party software, including 'crawlers', bots, browser plug-ins, or browser extensions that scrape, modify the appearance of, or automate activity on LinkedIn's website." Violators "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down", and "any prohibited tools they're using may become non-operational without notice." LinkedIn says it is "constantly working to improve our technical measures and defenses against … scraping, automation, and other tools". — [LinkedIn Help: Prohibited software and extensions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)

**hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn (2017–2022)**
- Aug 2017: N.D. Cal. granted hiQ a preliminary injunction. **Sept 9, 2019**: the Ninth Circuit affirmed. **June 14, 2021**: the Supreme Court vacated and remanded in light of *Van Buren v. United States*. **April 18, 2022**: on remand, the Ninth Circuit reaffirmed that scraping publicly available data likely does not violate the CFAA. **Nov 2022**: the district court ruled that hiQ breached LinkedIn's User Agreement. **Dec 2022**: settlement. — [Wikipedia: hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn)
- The Nov 2022 ruling held that the user agreement's bans on scraping and fake profiles are enforceable as a breach-of-contract claim. hiQ was liable for (1) automated scraping and (2) hiring crowd-workers ("turkers") to create fake accounts. Lesson drawn: a company is liable when its agents or contractors violate a site's terms on its behalf. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/trials-amp-appeals-amp-compensation/1259958/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case)
- The parties filed a stipulated consent judgment on **Dec 6, 2022**, and the court entered the Consent Judgment and Permanent Injunction on **Dec 8, 2022**. hiQ agreed to stop using automated means to access LinkedIn, to delete data collected without permission (ZwillGen adds source code and algorithms), and to pay **$500,000**. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/trials-amp-appeals-amp-compensation/1259958/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case); [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/); LinkedIn press: [LinkedIn wins hiQ lawsuit](https://news.linkedin.com/2022/december/hiqlinkedinlawsuit-)
- Note a conflict: ZwillGen's summary (as extracted) describes April 2022 as the Ninth Circuit "dissolving" the injunction, while Wikipedia says the Ninth Circuit *reaffirmed* the injunction in April 2022. The Wikipedia version matches the widely reported holding, but verify against the opinion if it matters.

**Meta v. Bright Data (logged-out scraping), Jan 23, 2024**
- Judge Edward Chen (N.D. Cal.) granted Bright Data summary judgment on breach of contract: "The Facebook and Instagram Terms do not bar logged-off scraping of public data." Browsing a public page without an account or clickwrap does not create a contract. — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [MediaPost, Jan 25, 2024](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/392920/judge-sides-against-meta-in-battle-over-scraping-p.html?edition=)

**LinkedIn v. Proxycurl (Nubela), Jan–Jul 2025**
- LinkedIn filed a federal suit in **January 2025** for user-agreement violations. The complaint alleged Nubela/Proxycurl created hundreds of thousands of fake LinkedIn accounts, scraped millions of profiles including non-public data, and resold the data through the Proxycurl API. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/); [Cleanlist (secondary)](https://www.cleanlist.ai/alternatives/proxycurl)
- Resolution (reported **July 2025**): the court entered a **permanent injunction**. Proxycurl must permanently delete all LinkedIn data obtained through unauthorized means, stop accessing LinkedIn, and send the injunction to its customers. Quote from LinkedIn legal VP Sarah Wight (spelled "Wright" in the article): "We've now resolved this case in a way that holds Proxycurl accountable." — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/)
- Proxycurl shut down on **July 4, 2025**. Its CEO Steven Goh said he had built a ~$10M business and cited Microsoft's "unlimited war chest". — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/); [LinkedAPI guide (secondary)](https://linkedapi.io/guides/proxycurl-alternatives)

**LinkedIn v. ProAPIs (filed Oct 2025, pending as far as found)**
- Filed around **Oct 6, 2025** in N.D. Cal. against ProAPIs and its CEO Rahmat Alam. LinkedIn alleges "millions" of fake accounts ("hundreds if not thousands of new accounts per day"), each scraping hundreds of profiles before detection, sold for up to **$15,000/month**. Claims: User Agreement breach, CFAA, circumvention of technical measures. Relief sought: damages and a permanent injunction. — [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/183001/security/linkedin-sues-proapis-for-15k-month-linkedin-data-scraping-scheme.html); [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/news/linkedin-sues-firm-for-scraping-millions-of-linkedin-member-profiles/). (The case number printed by Security Affairs looks malformed; treat it as unverified.)

**Enforcement against auto-apply tools and accounts**
- A LinkedIn spokesperson told 404 Media (2024) that LinkedIn knew about the open-source auto-apply bot AIHawk and that "automated tools are not allowed on its platform". At least one user reported a ban "due to the use of AI Hawk for automating applications". The AIHawk repo was archived on **Apr 16, 2026**. — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/email/df2c731a-b61b-4ed1-91c1-10874e9efe9e/); [GitHub AIHawk README](https://github.com/feder-cr/linkedIn_auto_jobs_applier_with_AI/blob/main/README.md)
- Vendor and blog sources (not primary) say LinkedIn has "aggressively" cracked down since late 2025, flagging accounts and restricting Easy Apply. Reported triggers: more than ~25–30 applications/day, identical answers, DOM-manipulation fingerprints from extensions, and inhuman click speed. — [Cleverly blog](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/why-linkedin-automation-tools-get-your-account-banned-and-what-to-do-instead); [jobapplyai.in](https://jobapplyai.in/blog/is-auto-applying-linkedin-jobs-against-tos/). LinkedIn publishes no official numbers.
- Vendor sources report LinkedIn "clamped down on PhantomBuster's IPs". — [Northlight blog](https://northlight.ai/blog/is-linkedin-automation-against-the-rules) (unverified, secondary)

**Official LinkedIn APIs and partner programs**
- Since 2015 LinkedIn API access generally requires joining a partner program. The Job Posting API is "restricted to developers approved by LinkedIn", who must sign an API agreement. Approved ATS and HR platforms can post jobs and receive applications. — [LinkedIn Job Posting dev tools README](https://github.com/linkedin-developers/job-posting-development-tools/blob/master/README.md); [Microsoft Learn: Job Posting customer configuration](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/talent/job-postings/api/job-posting-customer-configuration?view=li-lts-2025-10)
- **Apply Connect** is an ATS-partner integration that lets candidates apply on LinkedIn while applications flow into the partner ATS (e.g., Greenhouse supports it). It is employer and ATS side, not available to job-seeker tools. — [Microsoft Learn: Apply Connect overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/talent/apply-connect?view=li-lts-2025-10); [Greenhouse support: LinkedIn Apply Connect](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/17073067102747-LinkedIn-Apply-Connect-integration); [LinkedIn ATS partners](https://business.linkedin.com/talent-solutions/ats-partners)
- Secondary sources claim that Talent Solutions partner approval takes 3–6 months with under 10% approved. — [scale.jobs blog](https://scale.jobs/blog/linkedin-api-integration-with-ats-step-by-step-guide) (unverified figure)

### Inferences
- **Three different legal theories.** (a) CFAA, US federal "hacking" law: weak against logged-out public scraping (hiQ 2022) and, after Amazon v. Perplexity (Aug 2026, §5), weak against a developer whose agent acts in the user's own session under the user's direction. (b) Contract (User Agreement): strong against anyone logged in, i.e., the *user's* own account, and against a company acting through users or fake accounts (hiQ "agents" holding). (c) Account enforcement: LinkedIn can restrict or ban accounts with no lawsuit at all. This is the most immediate risk to end users of an auto-apply product.
- **Logged-out guest job pages.** Under Meta v. Bright Data, terms likely don't bind a logged-out server-side reader. But LinkedIn's terms say "scrape or copy the Services", and the company has sued even when the data was public. Reading guest job pages from a commercial server at scale is legally gray and will likely draw technical blocking (IP rate limits). It is the kind of thing LinkedIn chose to litigate in hiQ, Proxycurl and ProAPIs. All three involved fake accounts or logged-in scraping, and that is what turned "gray" into "liable".
- **Inside the user's session.** A browser extension or agent that submits Easy Apply inside the user's session is plainly within the §8.2 "bots … automated methods" and Help Center "browser extensions that … automate activity" language. The legal exposure falls mainly on the *user* (breach of contract, account ban). A vendor that markets the tool could face contract-interference or unfair-competition claims, which the Ninth Circuit expressly left open in Amazon v. Perplexity. Human approval per submission and human-paced volumes lower the ban risk but do not make the activity compliant.
- **Don't build on a LinkedIn API.** No official route exists for a job-seeker product to search LinkedIn jobs or apply via API, so "LinkedIn integration" in a hosted product would be either scraping or user-session automation.

### Gaps
- Not found: an official LinkedIn statement or primary data on auto-apply enforcement numbers (ban counts, Easy Apply limits) for 2025–2026. The figures above come from vendor blogs.
- The current status of LinkedIn v. ProAPIs (any ruling or settlement after Oct 2025) was not found.
- Not found: any LinkedIn lawsuit aimed specifically at an auto-apply *job-seeker* tool (as opposed to data scrapers or resellers).
- No primary source found confirming whether LinkedIn's current terms mention "apply for jobs" specifically among prohibited automated actions; the quoted §8.2 text lists contacts, messages and posts.

## 2. ATS job boards: public APIs, terms, third-party application submission, bot protection

### Takeaway
Reading jobs is easy and sanctioned: Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters and Comeet expose public, keyless (or public-token) job-board endpoints, and Workday's careers site has an undocumented keyless JSON endpoint. *Submitting* applications via API is designed for the employer, not third parties. Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Workable all require the employer's secret API key, and Lever requires partner-program OAuth for multi-customer products. So a third-party auto-apply product cannot legitimately use the apply APIs. It must fill the public web form, which is where CAPTCHA, reCAPTCHA v3 scoring and new fraud tooling sit.

### Cited Findings
**Greenhouse**
- "Job Board data is publicly available, so authentication is not required for any GET endpoints." Only `POST https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{board_token}/jobs/{id}` (application submission) requires Basic Auth with the employer's Job Board API key (Base64). — [Greenhouse Job Board API docs](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html)
- Greenhouse warns that a direct client-side POST "would reveal your secret key to anybody that views source". Submissions must be "proxied by your own servers". Greenhouse encourages customers to use the Embedded Job Application because of its "built-in spam protection measures". Greenhouse does not validate required fields on API submissions. — [Greenhouse Job Board API docs](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html); [JobsPipe (secondary)](https://jobspipe.dev/sources/greenhouse)

**Lever**
- Public, unauthenticated `GET /v0/postings/SITE` and `/v0/postings/SITE/POSTING-ID` (JSON, HTML, iframe). The apply POST needs an API key generated by "a Super Admin of your account". Rate limit: 429 above **2 application POSTs per second**. Lever recommends "directing candidates to Lever's hosted application form" and advises implementers to "Implement spam mitigations such as captchas and session or IP based rate limits." A consent field for candidates rolled out from mid-Sept 2024. — [Lever postings-api (GitHub)](https://github.com/lever/postings-api)
- Products connecting to multiple Lever customers "must go through the partner program and use OAuth". — [100hires Lever API guide (secondary)](https://100hires.com/lever-api.html)

**Ashby**
- Public Job Postings API: `GET https://api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/{JOB_BOARD_NAME}?includeCompensation=true`, no auth. It returns title, location, department, description, workplace type, apply URL and optional compensation tiers. Unlisted jobs (`isListed:false`) "should only be available via direct link". — [Ashby: public job posting API](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/docs/public-job-posting-api)
- Application submission (`applicationForm.submit`) requires an API key with the `candidatesWrite` permission, i.e., the employer's key. — [Ashby API reference: applicationForm.submit](https://developers.ashbyhq.com/reference/applicationformsubmit)

**SmartRecruiters**
- Public Posting API (`api.smartrecruiters.com/posting-api/v1`). The Application API (`POST /postings/:uuid/candidates`, `GET /postings/:uuid/configuration` for screening questions) is offered to "customers and partners", with job boards named as a use case. "The operation is done on behalf of a candidate … candidate can later on see and edit this data." — [SmartRecruiters Application API](https://developers.smartrecruiters.com/docs/application-api-1); [SmartRecruiters Post an Application](https://developers.smartrecruiters.com/docs/post-an-application)

**Workable**
- `POST https://{subdomain}.workable.com/spi/v3/jobs/{shortcode}/candidates` creates an applied candidate (`"sourced": false`). It uses the account's API token, and Workable runs a partner program for integrations. — [Workable API reference](https://workable.readme.io/reference/job-candidates-create); [Workable developers / partner program](https://www.workable.com/developers); [Workable help: careers page via API](https://help.workable.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012771647-Using-the-Workable-API-to-create-a-careers-page)

**Comeet (now Spark Hire Recruit)**
- The Careers API is a "public, tokenized REST API" scoped per customer by company UID plus a public token, for powering careers sites and syndicating to job boards. It reads one customer at a time. — [Comeet Careers API overview](https://developers.comeet.com/reference/careers-api-overview); [api-evangelist/comeet](https://github.com/api-evangelist/comeet)

**Workday**
- There is no documented public job-feed API. Careers sites call an unauthenticated JSON endpoint: `POST https://<tenant>.<wdN>.myworkdayjobs.com/wday/cxs/<tenant>/<site>/jobs`, with a page-size cap of 20. It is widely used by third-party scrapers. — [JobsPipe Workday guide (secondary)](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/workday-api-guide); [FetchSmith (secondary)](https://fetchsmith.com/blog/workday-career-site-json-api)

**Bot detection and CAPTCHA on application forms**
- Vendor reports: "Workday and some Greenhouse tenants intermittently add captcha walls", and reCAPTCHA v3 scores silently. In a vendor test of 8 auto-apply bots (May 2026), only 3 actually clicked Submit. Failures clustered on Workday (4) and Greenhouse (3), plus one LinkedIn CAPTCHA. — [FastApply blog (vendor, low reliability)](https://blog.fastapply.co/ai-job-application-bots-which-actually-submit-2026); [ApplyGoat blog (vendor)](https://applygoat.com/blogs/auto-apply-jobs-without-bot-detection)
- Greenhouse Real Talent analyzes phone, email, IP and location signals to flag spam and bot submissions (details in §3). — [Greenhouse newsroom](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/greenhouse-real-talent-tm-launches-to-fix-overwhelming-candidate-pipelines-while-combatting-fraud-and-spam-in-hiring)

### Inferences
- **Reading jobs from ATS boards is the lowest-risk data source for a product.** These endpoints exist so jobs get syndicated, and they need no account, contract acceptance or login. Remaining risks are rate-limiting and each vendor's site terms; I did not find any of these vendors prohibiting third-party reading of public boards.
- **Submitting via the employer's apply API is not an option.** It needs the employer's secret key (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable), and using a leaked or harvested key would be unauthorized access. The legitimate API route would be ATS partner programs (Lever OAuth partner, SmartRecruiters partner, Workable partner), which target job boards and sourcing tools. Getting approved as a mass "auto-apply" source is unlikely given the employer backlash in §3.
- **Form-filling in the user's browser therefore remains the only practical path.** That is also where reCAPTCHA, Real Talent-style IP and identity signals, and per-tenant Workday account creation create friction. Human-in-the-loop (the user solves the CAPTCHA and clicks Submit) is both more robust and more defensible.

### Gaps
- Not found: an explicit statement in Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby terms on whether third-party tools may submit applications through the *hosted* form on a candidate's behalf. The docs only address customers' own custom forms.
- No primary Workday documentation found on CAPTCHA or bot protection for candidate applications, or on Workday's terms for third-party reading of `/wday/cxs/`.
- SmartRecruiters' exact partner-approval criteria for the Application API were not on the fetched page.

## 3. Employer and ATS backlash against AI auto-apply (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Application volume has surged, and AI is a major cited cause: LinkedIn saw 11,000 applications/minute (+45% YoY) by mid-2025. ATS vendors now sell spam, bot and fraud detection plus identity verification (Greenhouse Real Talent, announced June 2025, GA Feb 2026; partnered with CLEAR). Recruiter and candidate trust is low. The backlash targets mass, generic and deceptive applications (hidden prompt injections, fake identities), not AI-assisted tailoring as such.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn averages **11,000 applications per minute, up 45% year over year**, per The New York Times (June 2025), which said generative AI tools contribute to "the deluge". One HR consultant pulled a remote role after 1,200+ applications. — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-job-applications-linkedin/); [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/ai-job-applications-flood-linkedin-with-11000-per-minute/)
- **Greenhouse Real Talent**, announced **June 3, 2025**, combines AI talent filtering, "spam and fraud detection using sophisticated algorithms to identify bot submissions and fraudulent patterns", and cheating and misrepresentation detection. It cites Gartner's forecast that 25% of applicants will be fake by 2028 and DOJ cases of 300+ US firms hit by foreign-operative hiring scams. — [Greenhouse newsroom](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/greenhouse-real-talent-tm-launches-to-fix-overwhelming-candidate-pipelines-while-combatting-fraud-and-spam-in-hiring)
- Greenhouse partnered with CLEAR for identity verification of applicants (2025). — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91350897/greenhouse-and-clear-team-up-to-fight-fake-job-applications-flooding-tech-hiring)
- Real Talent reportedly became generally available in **February 2026**. It analyzes phone numbers, emails, IP and location data and shows "explainable risk indicators" rather than auto-rejecting. Reported results: 10.2M fraud checks, ~1 in 4 candidates flagged for review, 1 in 33 failing verification. — [HR Tech Edge](https://hrtechedge.com/hr/greenhouses-real-talent-targets-ai-era-hiring-fraud/) (search-snippet level; figures are Greenhouse's own)
- The Greenhouse 2025 Workforce Report says more than 1 in 5 US candidates have used AI agents to apply automatically. — [Greenhouse blog: hiring pipeline overload](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/hiring-pipeline-overload) (seen in search snippet; not fetched)
- Greenhouse "AI in Hiring" report (Nov 20, 2025; 4,100+ respondents in the US, UK, Ireland and Germany): 49% of candidates are applying to more jobs than a year earlier; **41% admit using prompt injections** (hidden text in résumés to bypass AI filters); 65% of hiring managers caught dishonest AI use; 46% of US job seekers say trust in hiring has fallen (42% blame AI); only 8% think AI makes hiring fairer; only 50% of recruiters think AI improved hiring. — [HR Tech Edge summary of Greenhouse report](https://hrtechedge.com/greenhouse-report-exposes-a-hiring-system-in-crisis-ai-hacks-fraud-fears-ghost-jobs-and-collapsing-trust/)
- NACE (career services body) published on "AI fraud, auto-apply" and authentic career readiness, showing that university career centers are also pushing back. — [NACE](https://www.naceweb.org/career-readiness/best-practices/the-ghostwritten-candidate-ai-fraud-auto-apply-and-the-fight-for-authentic-career-readiness)
- LinkedIn's own counter-move is employer-side AI: "Hiring Assistant", an agent that screens candidates and messages applicants (launched Oct 2024, expanded since). — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-job-applications-linkedin/); [TechCrunch, Jan 15, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/15/linkedin-ads-free-ai-tools-for-job-hunters-and-recruiters/?stream=top)

### Inferences
- **What is being punished is volume, sameness and deception.** IP and identity signals, velocity, duplicate answers and prompt injections are what Real Talent-type tools and LinkedIn are built to catch. A product that sends few, truthful, tailored applications from the user's own browser, identity and IP looks like a normal candidate. That is a positioning advantage, but only if the product never offers mass mode.
- **The arms race favors the employer side.** Each new fraud layer (identity verification, IP risk scoring) raises the cost and lowers the success rate of server-side or proxy-based auto-apply.

### Gaps
- No "platform ban of auto-apply tools" by an ATS vendor (e.g., Greenhouse blocking a named auto-apply company) was found in 2024–2026. The only enforcement found is LinkedIn account-level action plus statements.
- Not found: independent (non-vendor) data on interview rates for auto-applied vs. manual applications.

## 4. Commercial job-data APIs and aggregators (alternatives to scraping)

### Takeaway
The legal job feeds now come from third-party aggregators (TheirStack, Fantastic.jobs, Coresignal, JSearch/OpenWeb Ninja, Adzuna) at roughly $25–$1,500+/month. The platforms themselves don't offer one: Indeed's Publisher/Job Search API is gone, Google has no public "Google for Jobs" API, and LinkedIn has none. Many aggregators source part of their data by scraping LinkedIn, Indeed or Google Jobs, so licensing from them moves the risk upstream rather than removing it. Adzuna's terms restrict commercial reuse without a license.

### Cited Findings
- **Indeed**: Publisher sign-ups closed in 2020. The job-search API was deprecated in 2023 and switched off in 2024. Indeed's remaining APIs (Job Sync, Indeed Apply, Disposition Sync, Sponsored Jobs) are partner-only and employer-side, and none returns job search results. — [JobsPipe: Indeed Publisher API (secondary)](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/indeed-publisher-api); [RolesAPI (secondary)](https://rolesapi.com/blog/does-indeed-have-an-api/)
- **Adzuna**: permitted uses are "1. Publishing Adzuna ad listings 2. Publishing Jobsworth salary estimates 3. Personal research". Commercial organizations get a 14-day evaluation trial, after which "a licence agreement may be required". Default limits: 25 hits/min, 250/day, 1,000/week, 2,500/month. Attribution ("Jobs by Adzuna", min 116×23 px, linked) is required. — [Adzuna API Terms of Service](https://developer.adzuna.com/docs/terms_of_service)
- **JSearch (OpenWeb Ninja, also on RapidAPI)**: real-time postings "from Google for Jobs", which it describes as covering LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Monster and others. Plans: free (200 requests/month), Pro $25 (10,000 requests), Ultra $75, Mega $150, or pay-as-you-go at $0.005/request. — [OpenWeb Ninja JSearch](https://www.openwebninja.com/api/jsearch); [RapidAPI JSearch](https://rapidapi.com/letscrape-6bRBa3QguO5/api/jsearch/details); [JobsPipe (secondary)](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/jsearch-api-direct)
- **TheirStack**: claims 360k+ sources in 195 countries. Free tier: 200 API credits/month. Paid: $49/month (1,500 credits) up to $5,500/month (5M credits). 1 credit per job record, and credits roll over 12 months. — [TheirStack pricing](https://theirstack.com/en/pricing); [TheirStack plans docs](https://theirstack.com/en/docs/pricing/plans)
- **Fantastic.jobs**: 200k+ employers across 54 ATS platforms (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, BambooHR, etc.) plus job boards including LinkedIn, Wellfound and YC. About 3M career-site jobs and 11M job-board jobs per month, ATS polled hourly. Self-serve plans: $95/month (20k jobs) to $900/month (500k jobs). Also sold on Apify. — [Fantastic.jobs API](https://fantastic.jobs/api); [Fantastic.jobs about](https://fantastic.jobs/about); [Apify listing](https://apify.com/fantastic-jobs/career-site-job-listing-api)
- **Coresignal**: multi-source public web data, ~468M job postings in its dataset. API from $49/month, Pro $800, Premium $1,500, Growth $1,000 at $0.007/credit (1 credit per job). Bulk datasets cost more than $1,000/month. — [Coresignal pricing](https://coresignal.com/pricing/); [apiserpent (secondary)](https://apiserpent.com/blog/coresignal-pricing-explained); [Datarade](https://datarade.ai/data-providers/coresignal/profile)
- After Proxycurl's July 2025 shutdown, a crop of "LinkedIn scraping API" vendors markets replacements, which shows that much "LinkedIn data" on the market is scraped. — [api.market](https://api.market/blog/z-api-hub/z-linkedin/best-linkedin-scraping-api-2026); [Cleanlist](https://www.cleanlist.ai/alternatives/proxycurl)

### Inferences
- For a hosted product, the cleanest stack is: (1) direct public ATS board endpoints, for company-targeted search; (2) a licensed aggregator such as Fantastic.jobs or TheirStack (ATS-sourced) for broad discovery; (3) LinkedIn only as a link-out or user-side view, not ingested server-side.
- **Diligence on aggregators.** Check each one's sourcing and indemnity. If an aggregator's LinkedIn-sourced records come from logged-in or fake-account scraping, the Proxycurl injunction shows customers can be told to delete data (Proxycurl had to send the injunction to its customers).
- **Cost scale.** Covering one user's daily search is a few hundred to a few thousand records per month, which fits in the low tiers ($25–$100/month) and spreads across users. Volume pricing is in the $0.002–$0.007/job range.

### Gaps
- **Google for Jobs**: no official public API was found. Google Cloud Talent Solution is an employer-side job search engine, not a feed of Google's index (not verified this session). JSearch's "from Google for Jobs" implies scraping Google, and Google's ToS position on that was not researched.
- Not found: aggregator terms on redistribution to end users or display rights (e.g., whether TheirStack or Fantastic.jobs allow showing full descriptions in a consumer app).
- Pricing pages change often. The figures are from 2026 snippets and vendor pages, not contracts.

## 5. Browser automation at scale: server-side vs. extension, Chrome Web Store, account-ban risk, agentic browsers

### Takeaway
Server-side automation means the product logs into users' accounts from its own IPs or uses headless browsers. That carries the most legal and detection risk: it looks like the fake-account and headless scraping LinkedIn litigates, and it is what bot defenses target. An extension or agent working in the user's own browser is now meaningfully safer under US federal anti-hacking law, since the Ninth Circuit (Aug 4, 2026, Amazon v. Perplexity) held that *the user*, not the agent developer, "accesses" the site. Contract, unfair-competition and tortious-interference claims stay open, and account bans by the site remain fully available. The Chrome Web Store allows such extensions but requires user confirmation before sending messages on the user's behalf, narrow single purpose, prominent data disclosure, and no remotely hosted code.

### Cited Findings
**Amazon v. Perplexity (Comet) is the key agentic-browser case**
- **Nov 2025**: Amazon sued, alleging computer fraud because Comet disguised itself as standard Chrome and refused to identify itself as an AI agent while shopping in users' accounts. — [CNBC, Mar 10, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/10/amazon-wins-court-order-to-block-perplexitys-ai-shopping-agent.html); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/judge-blocks-perplexitys-ai-bot-from-shopping-on-amazon-in-early-test-of-agentic-commerce/)
- **March 2026**: Judge Maxine M. Chesney (N.D. Cal.) granted a preliminary injunction barring Comet from Amazon accounts, finding Comet accessed accounts "with the Amazon user's permission, but without authorization by Amazon". She also ordered destruction of collected Amazon data. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/10/amazon-wins-court-order-to-block-perplexitys-ai-shopping-agent.html); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/amazon-wins-preliminary-injunction-against-perplexitys-comet/569256/)
- **Aug 4, 2026**: the Ninth Circuit reversed, finding the CFAA claim unlikely to succeed: "it is the user who 'accesses' Amazon's computers, with the help of the Assistant". It also said "The CFAA's plain language suggests the Assistant itself cannot 'access'". It cautioned that "on a different record or new facts, Perplexity may exercise control over the Assistant in such a way as to gain entry". The ruling "does not preclude other theories of liability, including breach of contract, unfair competition, tortious interference, or state consumer protection claims." The case continues in district court. — [Troutman Pepper Privacy](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/08/ninth-circuit-holds-human-user-not-developer-of-ai-agent-responsible-for-websites-accessed/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2230471/perplexity-has-successfully-overturned-amazon-injunction-on-its-ai-shopping-bot/); [Dataconomy, Aug 5, 2026](https://dataconomy.com/2026/08/05/perplexity-amazon-injunction-court-ruling/)

**Chrome Web Store program policies**
- "Extensions may only collect, use, or transmit user data that is necessary for the extension's disclosed single purpose" (Limited Use). If user data is handled, the extension "must prominently disclose what user data will be collected and how it will be used" before installation. "An extension must have a single purpose that is narrow and easy to understand." Under MV3, "the full functionality of an extension must be easily discernible from its submitted code", so remotely hosted code is barred. — [Chrome Web Store Program Policies](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/policies)
- Spam and Abuse: the store does not allow "extensions that send messages on behalf of the user without giving the user the ability to confirm the content and intended recipients." — [Chrome Web Store: Spam and Abuse](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/spam-and-abuse)

**How sites identify and respond to agents**
- Cloudflare proposed Web Bot Auth (HTTP message signatures, an IETF draft) in May 2025 and shipped "signed agents" in Aug 2025. The first cohort included ChatGPT agent, Block's Goose, Browserbase and Anchor Browser. Site owners can then allow only verified agents and block unverified automation. — [Cloudflare blog: signed agents](https://blog.cloudflare.com/signed-agents/)
- LinkedIn's terms make no exception for agentic browsers. Vendor guides say consumer agent browsers (Comet, ChatGPT Atlas, Claude, Gemini) "don't remove the LinkedIn problem" and that LinkedIn invested in behavioral monitoring in 2025–2026. — [HeroHunt (secondary)](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/browser-ai-agents-for-sourcing-2026-guide/); [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)
- Account-ban signals reported for extensions: forensic traces (DOM manipulation patterns, API-call sequences unlike native behavior) and velocity. — [Cleverly (vendor)](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/why-linkedin-automation-tools-get-your-account-banned-and-what-to-do-instead)

### Inferences
- **Server-side automation** (product-controlled headless browsers using user credentials or cookies) is the riskiest design for three reasons. The vendor arguably "controls" access, which is the Ninth Circuit's open door. It collects passwords or session tokens, a security and privacy liability. And datacenter IPs plus identical fingerprints are exactly what LinkedIn and Real Talent-style systems flag.
- **Extension or agent in the user's browser, user-directed, with per-application approval** is the design the Ninth Circuit's reasoning favors and that Chrome's "confirm before sending on behalf of the user" rule effectively requires. It still breaches LinkedIn's terms on LinkedIn specifically, so a product should either exclude LinkedIn auto-submission (link out and let the user click) or clearly warn users of the account risk.
- **Agent disclosure is cutting both ways.** Signed-agent standards let sites allow or deny AI agents explicitly, and Amazon's complaint centered on Comet hiding that it was an agent. A product that self-identifies won't be seen as deceptive, but sites that choose to block AI agents can do so outright.

### Gaps
- No public statement found from LinkedIn on how it treats ChatGPT Atlas, Comet or Claude in Chrome sessions specifically. Also not verified: a secondary claim that OpenAI removed "ChatGPT agent" in Aug 2026 ([HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/browser-ai-agents-for-sourcing-2026-guide/)).
- No Chrome Web Store enforcement case found involving a job auto-apply extension.
- Not researched: Firefox and Edge add-on store policies; Apple and Google mobile store rules.

## 6. Privacy and regulation for CVs and personal data (GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, Israel Amendment 13, EU AI Act, FTC)

### Takeaway
A hosted product becomes a controller of CVs, contact data, work history, sometimes sensitive data (disability, ethnicity, salary), and application logs. GDPR applies to EU users regardless of where the company sits. CCPA/CPRA applies above its revenue and volume thresholds, with ADMT rules from 2027. Israel's Amendment 13 (in force Aug 14, 2025) brings GDPR-like duties and large administrative fines. The EU AI Act's high-risk "employment" category (Annex III §4) targets systems used *for recruitment or selection*, i.e., employer or recruiter-side, so a job-seeker-side tool is very likely not high-risk. In any case, those obligations are now deferred to Dec 2, 2027. In the US, the FTC polices AI capability claims under Section 5 ("AI-washing"), but rolled back the "means and instrumentalities" theory for AI writing tools in Dec 2025.

### Cited Findings
**EU AI Act**
- Annex III point 4 (high-risk): "AI systems intended to be used for the recruitment or selection of natural persons, in particular to place targeted job advertisements, to analyse and filter job applications, and to evaluate candidates; AI systems intended to be used to make decisions affecting terms of work-related relationships…" — [AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- Timing: the Council and Parliament reached provisional agreement on **May 7, 2026**, and the Council gave final approval on **June 29, 2026** to the Digital Omnibus. This moves stand-alone Annex III high-risk obligations, recruitment included, from **Aug 2, 2026 to Dec 2, 2027**, and Annex I embedded AI to Aug 2, 2028. — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/eu-legislators-agree-to-delay-for-highrisk-ai-rules)

**GDPR (EU/EEA users)**
- GDPR applies to non-EU controllers that offer services to people in the EU (Art. 3(2)). Processing needs a lawful basis (Art. 6, typically contract with the user). Special-category data such as health/disability, ethnicity or union membership needs an Art. 9 condition, typically explicit consent. Solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects are restricted (Art. 22), which matters mainly on the employer side. Processors such as LLM API providers need Art. 28 contracts, and transfers outside the EU need Chapter V safeguards. — [GDPR Art. 3](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/); [Art. 6](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/); [Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/); [Art. 22](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-22-gdpr/); [Art. 28](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-28-gdpr/)

**California (CCPA/CPRA)**
- The CPPA's regulations on automated decision-making technology (ADMT), risk assessments and cybersecurity audits were approved by OAL on **Sept 23, 2025**. They phase in from **Jan 1, 2026**. ADMT duties for "significant decisions" apply from **Jan 1, 2027**. First risk-assessment filings are due **Apr 1, 2028**, and cybersecurity audits (revenue over $100M) also by Apr 1, 2028. — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/10/california-finalizes-cppa-regulations); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/cppa-finalizes-rules-admt-risk-assessments-and-cybersecurity-audits-requirements); [FPF brief](https://fpf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/FPF_CCPA-Regulations-Issue-Brief.pdf)

**Israel: Privacy Protection Law, Amendment 13**
- In force **Aug 14, 2025**. It broadens the definitions of "personal information" and "processing" toward GDPR, expands the Privacy Protection Authority's (PPA) enforcement and administrative-fine powers, and provides advance rulings within ~60 days. The PPA said it intends to use its powers "to their full extent" once the amendment takes effect. — [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/major-amendment-to-israeli-privacy-law-set-to-take-effect/)
- DPO is mandatory for public bodies, data brokers (10,000+ records), entities doing "systematic large-scale monitoring", and those processing sensitive data at large scale. Database registration is now required only for data brokers and public bodies. The old 72-hour breach-notice convention is replaced by "immediate" reporting of severe incidents. Fines run from "hundreds of thousands to millions of shekels". — [DPO Israel guide](https://dpoisrael.com/en/amendment-13/); [INPLP](https://inplp.com/latest-news/article/new-amendment-to-israeli-privacy-law-and-mandatory-dpo-appointment/)
- Other summaries: fines "into the millions of shekels (USD 500,000+)", doubled for repeat violations within two years. The amendment also expands breach-notice duties and data-subject rights, extends the limitation period, and introduces exemplary (statutory) damages. — [Multilaw Israel guide](https://www.multilaw.com/Multilaw/Multilaw/Data_Protection_Laws_Guide/DataProtection_Guide_Israel.aspx); [Chambers 2026 Israel](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/data-protection-privacy-2026/israel)

**FTC and consumer protection (US)**
- The FTC treats AI capability claims under Section 5: they must be truthful and substantiated. "Operation AI Comply" (from Sept 2024) continued under the new administration, with about a dozen "AI-washing" cases in 2025 covering overstated AI capabilities and unsubstantiated earnings claims. One 2025 case targeted agentic-AI productivity claims. — [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus/); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/ftc-brings-dozen-ai-washing-enforcement-cases-2025-targeting-overstated-ai); [DLA Piper, Aug 2025](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2025/08/ftcs-latest-ai-washing-case)
- **Rytr**: in its 2024 order the FTC treated an AI writing tool as providing the "means and instrumentalities" for deception (fake reviews). On **Dec 22, 2025** the FTC reopened and set aside that order, saying the complaint "failed to satisfy the legal requirements of the FTC Act" and that the order burdened AI innovation under the AI Action Plan. — [FTC press release, Dec 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-reopens-sets-aside-rytr-final-order-response-trump-administrations-ai-action-plan); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/ftc-reverses-rytr-consent-order-amid-push-for-fede)
- Job-scam context: FTC job-scam reports topped 105,000 in 2024, with losses over $513M (vs. $90M in 2020). This is the environment a paid job tool's marketing will be judged in. — [Defend-ID blog citing FTC data](https://blog.defend-id.com/2026/04/29/ai-job-scams-2026/) (secondary; check against FTC Consumer Sentinel)
- No FTC action specifically against an AI job-search or auto-apply tool was found.

### Inferences
- **The AI Act most likely doesn't classify a job-seeker-side tool as high-risk.** Matching jobs to a user, tailoring their CV and filling forms is not "recruitment or selection" by or for a recruiter. It would move into scope if the product added employer-facing features such as ranking or screening candidates, or targeting job ads at people. General transparency duties (e.g., disclosing AI interaction) and GDPR still apply. This is an inference; no regulator guidance on seeker-side tools was found.
- **Privacy design implications.** Local-first processing (today's kit) avoids most controller duties. A hosted version needs: a privacy notice, DPAs with LLM and hosting vendors, EU transfer mechanisms, deletion and export, breach response (immediate notice in Israel), and minimizing special-category fields (EEO, disability, veteran questions) or keeping them on-device. Under Amendment 13, holding many users' CVs could raise DPO questions if seen as "systematic monitoring". The "data broker" label applies only if data is provided to others as a business, which a product should avoid.
- **US marketing claims are a live risk.** Claims like "get 10x more interviews" or "AI applies while you sleep" need substantiation under Section 5 and state UDAP laws. The Rytr reversal lowers *federal* risk for "tool could be misused" theories, but not for the company's own claims, and state AGs remain active.

### Gaps
- Exact Amendment 13 fine amounts (NIS figures, per-record formulas) were not confirmed from a primary source (the Knesset text or the PPA).
- CCPA applicability thresholds for 2026 (inflation-adjusted revenue) were not verified this session.
- Not researched: other US state privacy laws (e.g., Colorado AI Act, now delayed) and the EU AI Act Art. 50 timeline under the Omnibus.

## 7. Liability for AI-fabricated CV content: why "never invent experience" matters

### Takeaway
Misstated credentials expose the *candidate* to rejection, termination (offer letters commonly make material misrepresentation grounds for firing even after hire) and, in egregious cases, fraud claims. Employers are actively hunting for AI misrepresentation: 65% of hiring managers report catching dishonest AI use. For the vendor, a tool that fabricates or inflates claims creates deceptive-practice exposure (its own marketing, and possibly facilitation theories at state level or abroad), reputational risk, and user-harm claims. A verifiable "never invent" guarantee is both a risk control and a differentiator.

### Cited Findings
- A candidate can be rejected for material misrepresentation "regardless of whether AI was used to create it". Most offer letters and employment agreements make it grounds for termination even after hire. Synthetic résumés with fabricated credentials "can violate fraud statutes". — [Recruitics](https://www.recruitics.com/ai-fake-applicants); [Treegarden](https://treegarden.io/blog/ai-generated-resume-detection/) (practitioner blogs, not legal authority)
- Employers may face negligent-hiring liability when falsified qualifications get through in regulated sectors (healthcare, finance, education), which gives them a strong incentive to verify. — [Recruitics](https://www.recruitics.com/ai-fake-applicants)
- Greenhouse report (Nov 2025): 65% of hiring managers caught dishonest AI use (32% AI-generated scripts, 22% prompt injections, 18% deepfakes), and 41% of candidates admit to prompt injections. Greenhouse's Real Talent includes "cheating and misrepresentation detection". — [HR Tech Edge](https://hrtechedge.com/greenhouse-report-exposes-a-hiring-system-in-crisis-ai-hacks-fraud-fears-ghost-jobs-and-collapsing-trust/); [Greenhouse newsroom](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/greenhouse-real-talent-tm-launches-to-fix-overwhelming-candidate-pipelines-while-combatting-fraud-and-spam-in-hiring)
- Vendor-cited survey: "49% of business leaders have already extended a job offer to a candidate who misrepresented qualifications using AI tools". — [search result from resume-fraud vendor blogs, e.g., Avvanz](https://www.avvanz.com/blog/resume-fraud-2026-employer-prevention) (unverified origin)
- The FTC's (now vacated) Rytr theory shows regulators have tried to hold AI *writing tools* responsible for deceptive content their users generate. That federal theory was abandoned on Dec 22, 2025. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-reopens-sets-aside-rytr-final-order-response-trump-administrations-ai-action-plan)

### Inferences
- **The user carries the legal liability for a false CV.** The vendor's exposure is mostly (a) consumer-protection claims if it markets "optimized" CVs that in practice inflate claims, (b) contract and tort claims from users harmed by fabricated content (e.g., a rescinded offer), and (c) platform and ATS reputation. Being flagged as a source of fraudulent applications could trigger blocking by Real Talent-style tools.
- **Concrete design implications.** Ground every CV claim in user-approved facts, with an audit trail. Never use prompt-injection or hidden-text tricks (41% of candidates do, and ATS vendors now detect them). Keep human approval per submission. These match the open-source kit's existing "never invent experience" rule and its build-time checks.

### Gaps
- No court case was found where an AI CV tool vendor was sued over fabricated content in a user's CV.
- Not researched: criminal fraud precedents for CV lies by jurisdiction (e.g., UK Fraud Act "false representation" prosecutions, Israeli Penal Law). This needs a dedicated legal source.
